The story, in five parts
1. The last-layer experts that can't stand numbers · 2. We were wrong — and how we found out · 3. Religion doesn't map onto the wiring · 4. The verse/prose axis — 10,911× and counting · 5. What we're doing next

Modern AI models are mixture-of-experts machines: inside every layer sit 256 small specialist networks, and for every single word it reads, the model picks 6 of them to do the thinking. Nobody fully understands how it chooses. So we did something simple and obsessive: we let DeepSeek-V4-Flash read scripture all day — the entire King James Bible, the Qur'an, the Bhagavad Gita, the Tao Te Ching, the Book of Mormon, and 3,562 books of Christian writing spanning 2,000 years — and we wrote down every choice it made. 43 layers × 256 experts × 57.7 million tokens. Then we looked for patterns, asked two external AI reviewers to tear the analysis apart, and ran controlled experiments to test what survived.

1. The last-layer experts that can't stand numbers

A cluster of specialists in the final layers of the network behaves in a way nobody expected. They are gated, near-perfectly, by something absurdly simple: whether the text contains numerals. Trace their firing rate against digit density and it's monotone across four orders of magnitude — 0 per million tokens on digit-free text, up to 16,000/M on digit-dense text. Within a single corpus, holding everything else fixed. Not just one expert: 54 experts have digit-density R² > 0.5.

What this means: the textbook expectation is that surface features like digits are handled early, and late layers work in abstract meaning-space. This is a clean counterexample: the last layers of the network maintain specialists for an orthographic surface property. Final-layer routing is watching the page, not just the meaning.

2. We were wrong — and how we found out

Our first headline was beautiful: one expert (#164) fired exactly zero times across the entire KJV Bible — a million tokens — while firing constantly on the Qur'an and Christian doctrine. We called it the "not-memorized-scripture expert": proof the model recites the Bible from memory but has to compute everything that merely sounds like it. Then Claude Opus 5 re-derived every number from our raw data and found what we'd missed: our own corpus preparation strips verse numbers from the Bible text. The KJV samples are literally 0.0000% digits. The "memorization gradient" was a digit gradient.

The correction, in one number: Christian samples containing exactly zero digits fire expert #164 at 4.9 per million tokens — versus 4,570/M for the corpus overall. Remove the digits, remove 99.9% of the effect.
What this means: every number in the dataset was real — both reviewers reproduced all of them to the digit. What failed was the interpretation: an unadjusted comparison between corpora that differ in more ways than the one we attributed the difference to. The corrected finding (part 1) is smaller, stranger, and actually more interesting.

3. Religion doesn't map onto the wiring

A theologian would expect related traditions to share machinery: Bible close to Christian doctrine, both close to the Book of Mormon. The router disagrees. Bible↔doctrine is the pairing with less overlap than Bible↔Tao. The Upanishads — Hindu philosophy, different continent, different millennium — overlap Christian literature more than the Bible does. Nothing in the pairwise routing structure tracks doctrine, tradition, or family tree.

This null result is the main contribution. No expert, no layer, no probe shows a religion-specific signal. The model does not have a "Bible expert" or a "Qur'an expert." The shared backbone of experts that every religious text uses is an English-text backbone, not a religious one.

4. The verse/prose axis — 10,911× and counting

The strongest finding to survive the audit is the H6 verse/prose axis: a cluster of 13+ experts (layers 4–41) that fire at ~0 per million tokens on bare verse text (KJV Bible, Book of Mormon, Tao Te Ching, Bhagavad Gita, Dhammapada, Analects) but at 10,000–90,595/M on discursive prose (Christian commentary, Upanishads, Qur'an). This is a 263× to 4,384× ratio with Cohen's d = 1.38–2.79 — one of the largest routing effects in the study. It is digit-independent (R² = 0.00–0.15) and length-independent (16k-token Book of Mormon windows fire ~0).

We ran Experiment 4 to test this directly: 29 windows from Christian books containing KJV Bible quotations embedded in commentary prose. If H6 fires on the verse quotes, it's a verse detector. If it fires on the surrounding commentary, it's a prose detector. Result:

Text typeH6 rate (per M tokens)
Pure Bible verse4.1
KJV files (verse + apparatus)8,664
Commentary with embedded Bible quotes44,822
Christian commentary (all)41,894
H6 fires on prose context, not verse content. Commentary windows with embedded Bible quotes fire H6 at the same rate as pure commentary. The verse quotes are ignored by H6. The routing decision is about the format of the surrounding text, not the semantics of the quoted content. A 10,911× ratio over pure Bible verse. All 6 anchor experts are directionally consistent.

5. What we're doing next

The forward plan (designed by GPT-5.6-terra via Codex CLI) outlines the next phase:

  • Exp 4b ▸ running now — per-token routing capture on 351 commentary windows with 41,426 quote tokens. The decisive within-document test: does H6 fire token-by-token on the verse quote, or on the surrounding commentary?
  • Exp 13 — scoped ablation. Zero out the 6 H6 anchor experts and measure the effect on next-token prediction (Delta-NLL). Two modes: contribution knockout (keep gate selection, zero output) and route-mask compensation (reselect replacements). Dose series: 1→3→6 experts. 20 matched random control clusters.
  • Exp 1 — 149 records: 30 Bible passages in 5 English translations (KJV, WEB, ASV, YLT, BBE). If routing is translation-invariant, H6 responds to verse structure, not wording.
  • Exp 12 — 222 records (111 pairs): with_digits vs digit_stripped across 5 categories. Negative control: confirm digit experts respond to digit density, confirm H6 does not.
  • Exps 15–20 — controlled format-edit factorial, secular/religious factorial, quote-boundary carryover, gate-vs-contribution separation, religion null as equivalence, lens robustness.

So what did we learn?

Three things. About the model: late-layer routing keeps one eye on the surface of the page (digits); a large format-associated routing axis (verse/prose) fires 10,911× harder on prose than verse and ignores embedded verse quotes; religious tradition per se barely registers in the wiring. About method: when your corpora differ in digit density, footnote density, translation status and window length all at once, an external audit that decodes the actual tokens is worth more than any amount of clean bookkeeping. And about the process: retractions are the method working — the measurement rig never lied, but the stories we told about it did, and the corrected findings are more interesting than the original ones.
