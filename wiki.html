1 Abstract

We measure expert-router behaviour in DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 across nine religious corpora — the KJV Bible, Qur'an, Book of Mormon, Bhagavad Gita, Tao Te Ching, Dhammapada, Analects, Upanishads, and a 1,267-book corpus of historical Christian literature spanning patristics to modern devotion — totalling over 22 million tokens under a read-only, fail-closed harness. Three principal results emerge. (i) A shared scriptural backbone: a core of 19 layer-42 experts sits in the top-40 by frequency for every corpus, and pairwise top-expert overlap is high across all traditions (mean per-layer Jaccard 0.30–0.53), with the Book of Mormon closest to the Bible (0.53) — consistent with its explicit KJV lineage. (ii) A thin layer of hard specialists: individual experts act as binary tradition detectors (e.g. L42 e164: 0 activations per million tokens in the Bible vs 4,570 in Christian literature and 4,572 in the Qur'an; L41 e34: Bible 5.7 vs Qur'an 5,377 per million). Matched-n bootstrap confirms these are not small-sample artifacts. Crucially, the "Gita specialist" e164 and "Qur'an specialist" e34 both fire in post-biblical Christian doctrine at near-Qur'anic levels — reclassifying them as non-KJV-scripture experts rather than tradition-specific ones. (iii) Christian literature routes distinctly from the Bible itself (Jaccard 0.33, the lowest of any pair involving either), with divergence concentrated in layers 0–2 and 39–40, while mid layers (3–6, 19) share the most (up to 0.74) — doctrine activates the scriptural backbone but adds a heavy doctrinal overlay. Logit-lens decoding of Genesis 1 surfaces "Spirit" at 76.7% confidence at layer 30 — the KJV rendering of Genesis 1:2 — and bounded-Jacobian probes show layer-0 output influence ≈3.9× that of later layers.

2 Background and questions

DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 is a sparse mixture-of-experts model: each of 43 transformer layers routes every token to 6 of 256 experts through a learned gate. Interpretability of such routers is attractive because expert selection is discrete, countable, and reproducible — unlike dense activations it yields exact integer statistics that can be audited.

This study asks three questions:

  1. Backbone: do religious texts from different traditions share expert circuitry, or does each tradition route distinctly?
  2. Specialists: do individual experts discriminate traditions, and are such effects robust to corpus size?
  3. Scripture vs doctrine: does 2,000 years of Christian literature about scripture (theology, sermons, patristics) route like scripture itself?

3 Methods

3.1 Observation harness

Model weights (checkpoint rev 9e165c30) run tensor-parallel across two DGX Spark nodes with no modification; a hook on the router gate records, per token, the selected experts, gate weights, and per-expert activation norms. Every sample is appended to an fsynced JSONL with a fail-closed invariant: Σ expert_frequencies == seqlen × 6 on all 43 layers of every record, and (category, sample_index) keys must be unique. All 1,482 core records and all 1,267 wave-1 Christian records pass with zero violations.

3.2 REAP scoring

Experts are ranked by REAP (router-weighted expert activation product): the product of gate weight and activation norm per expert occurrence, aggregated per corpus. High-REAP experts are those the router both selects often and weights strongly. Text-level profiles use frequency-weighted mean REAP; comparisons use per-layer top-20 expert sets (expert IDs are layer-local and never pooled across layers).

3.3 Logit lens and bounded Jacobian

For J-lens probes, hidden states are projected through the model's own hc_head → RMSNorm → lm_head at each layer to decode intermediate token predictions. Because the model's custom fp4/fp8 kernels lack backward passes, Jacobian norms are estimated by deterministic finite differences along 16 random projection directions per source layer (fixed seed), bounding each source layer's influence on the output state.

3.4 Robustness

All specialist claims are tested with matched-n bootstrap (5,000 resamples) at the smaller corpus's sample size, as reported in §5.4.

L42 e164 never fires once across all 1,189 Bible chapters (0 in ~1.05 M tokens; a per-million rate < 1) yet fires ~4,570 times per million tokens in both the Qur'an and 1,267 Christian books — a >1,000× contrast between two corpora of the same faith tradition. The dividing line the router learned is not "religion" or even "tradition," but something like textual register: KJV-1611 scripture English vs everything else. L41 e34 behaves identically (5.7 vs 5,377/5,161).

5.3 Doctrine routes differently from scripture

Finding 3. Christian literature is the Bible's least-similar comparator (Jaccard 0.33) despite shared subject matter. Divergence is concentrated in early layers (0–2: Jaccard ≤ 0.03) and layers 39–40 (0.11–0.14); mid layers 3–6 and 19 are most shared (0.54–0.74). A distinct set of "doctrinal overlay" experts carries the excess: e128 (176,511 vs 13,292 per 1 M tokens over the Bible), e142 (74,104 vs 755), e117 (64,276 vs 1,876), e201 (56,618 vs 1,305), e241, e93, e202.

Interpretation: mid-network lexical/semantic circuitry recognizes shared religious vocabulary and narrative; the divergent early layers plausibly encode register/style (sermons, systematic theology, hymns vs narrative prose), and the divergent late layers encode task-level differences (exposition vs narration). No expert is wholly inactive in any corpus at any layer — all 256 experts per layer fire somewhere — the routing differences are proportional, not binary, except for the specialists of §5.2.

5.4 Robustness (matched-n bootstrap)

  • L42 e164 — 0/1,189 Bible activations; all 5,000 bootstrap Bible subsets of n=18 give exactly 0. Not a small-n artifact. The Gita's magnitude (n=18) has a wide CI but the direction (zero vs nonzero) is certain.
  • L41 e34 — Bible 0.05 [0.01–0.09] vs Qur'an 24.2 [21.7–26.8] at matched n=115: a ~300× gap with non-overlapping intervals.
  • L34 e33 — Bible 2.7 [2.5–2.9] vs Dhammapada 4,358 [2,013–7,107] at n=26.
  • Upanishads (n=3) — all three samples activate e105 (~916); reported as hypothesis-grade only.

6 Discussion

What did the router learn about religion? Not theology, mostly. The dominant signal is register — the KJV's 1611 scripture English is a near-binary feature the router isolates into a handful of late-layer experts, fired by nothing else in 22 M observed tokens. Traditions that imitate the register (Book of Mormon) join the Bible's circuitry; traditions with different registers (Qur'an translations, doctrinal prose) route around it even when the subject matter is identical.

Scripture vs doctrine. The model treats "Christianity" as at least two regimes: narrative scripture and theological literature. The doctrinal overlay experts of §5.3 (e128, e142, e117, …) activate for exposition-about-scripture, not scripture — an operational, data-driven definition of "theological register."

Layer division of labour. Combining §5.3 and §5.6: layer 0 is the most output-leveraged but register-agnostic; layers 3–6 and 19 carry the shared religious-lexical backbone; layers 34–42 host the discriminative specialists and the KJV-register experts.

7 Limitations

  • Qur'an and Gita corpora are translation-English; register findings may partly measure translationese, not the source tradition.
  • Upanishads (n=3) and Gita (n=18) support directional claims only.
  • Christian wave 2 (2,295 books, 35.5 M tokens) and the full J-lens grid (~80 samples) are still running; §5.2–5.3 numbers are wave-1.
  • REAP is a router-statistic; causal claims about expert function would require activation patching, which this read-only protocol excludes.
  • Finite-difference Jacobians bound influence along 16 random directions only.

10 Addendum: routing concentration and depth (2026-08-15)

10.1 The New Testament is observed in full

The 16 k-token windowing applies only to the 3,705-book Christian-literature corpus. The Bible — including the entire NT — is observed chapter-by-chapter with no slicing: all 260 NT chapters, 235,902 tokens, verified against canonical chapter counts (Matthew 28 … Revelation 22); no NT chapter exceeds one 16 k window.

All corpora start near-uniform at layer 0 (~204–222 effective experts of 256) and concentrate monotonically with depth. Doctrinal prose (Christian literature) stays the most diffuse at every depth — exposition recruits a broader expert palette than scripture — while the Gita, the most formulaic corpus, collapses to 26 effective experts at L40. Top-8 experts absorb 58% of Qur'anic token-routings at L40 vs 30% for Christian literature.

10.3 What the observations contain

Per token position, per layer, the harness records: the 6 chosen expert IDs (from a 256-way softmax over router logits with sqrt-softplus scoring, rescaled ×1.5); the gate weight applied to each; each expert's FFN output norm ‖Wdown·σ(Wupx)⊙σ+(Wgatex)‖; and the residual-stream hidden state (4,096-dim) via the hyper-connection head for lens decoding. REAP per expert = gate weight × activation norm, aggregated. The weight matrices themselves (Wrouter ∈ ℝ4096×256 per layer; 256 expert FFN triples in fp4; hc_head; lm_head ∈ ℝ4096×129280) are never modified — the lens reuses the model's own read-out heads.

10.4 Specialist vs backbone firing style

When the L42 specialists fire they do so densely: e164 averages ~13 firings per Qur'an sample (up to 13 of every 6×seqlen routings) with mean activation norm ≈2,666 — comparable to backbone expert e26 (norm ≈1,590, ~19 firings/sample). Specialists are not rare, weak units; they are high-magnitude units gated off entirely outside their register.
