J-space lens (logit lens and bounded Jacobian)

J-space = the set of intermediate representations the model is "poised to verbalize" at each layer. 80 samples across all 8 traditions, complete.

1. What J-space measures

  • Logit lens — at each layer, project the residual stream through the unembedding matrix. Gives the model's running next-token guess before the final layer commits. Records top-10 tokens + probabilities per position per layer.
  • Bounded Jacobian — perturb the input embedding by ε=0.01 along 16 random directions, re-run, measure output logit change. L2 norm = cheap proxy for the full input-output Jacobian.

Finding (survives, with caveat): L0 output influence is ~2.5–3.5× that of later layers across all traditions. The caveat: only 16 projection directions, so these are approximate bounds, not exact Jacobian norms. Inter-tradition differences are modest and do not track REAP-based clustering.

3. Logit lens

Per-layer top-token evolution shows predictions stabilize around layer 30–35 across all traditions. No tradition shows qualitatively different lens behavior. See the interactive viewer for per-position per-layer inspection.

4. Interactive viewer

The J-space viewer (120KB, self-contained HTML) renders per-layer top-token evolution and bounded Jacobian sensitivity for all 80 samples. Select a tradition, sample, and position to see the model's running prediction at each layer.

5. Retracted logit-lens claim

Genesis 1:2 logit lens. Position 30 token is ' deep' not 'darkness' — the original claim that the lens predicted "darkness" at an early layer was based on a tokenization error.

6. Caveats and open problems

  • 16-projection bound is approximate, not exact Jacobian
  • Inter-tradition differences don't track REAP clustering — J-space may be sensitive to surface-form statistics rather than theological content
  • Digit-density stratification of J-space not yet done
