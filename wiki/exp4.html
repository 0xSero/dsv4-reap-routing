Experiment 4: Quotation Switch (Pilot + Exp 4b)

Within-document test: do H6 experts fire on quoted verse or surrounding commentary? Result: H6 fires on prose context, not verse quotes.

1. Design

30 windows (~16k tokens each) from Christian books containing KJV Bible quotations embedded in commentary prose. Each window contains both quoted verse and surrounding commentary. Question: does H6 fire on the verse portions, the commentary portions, or both?

5. Pilot findings

  1. H6 fires on prose context, not verse content. Commentary with quotes fires at 44,822/M — same as pure commentary. The verse quotes don't suppress H6.
  2. All 6 anchors fire more on commentary than KJV files. No anchor shows the opposite pattern.

6. Limitations (Exp 4 pilot)

  • Digit density confound (KJV files 2.3–6.1% digits vs commentary 0–1%)
  • Small n (30 windows, 5 KJV files)
  • No within-document quote-span annotation (pilot used window-level comparison)

7. Exp 4b: Scaled results (351 records)

Exp 4b addresses the pilot's limitations: 351 records, 5,156,659 tokens, 41,426 quote tokens across 18 sources, with annotated quote spans.

MetricValue
Records351
Tokens5,156,659
H6 rate (commentary prose)163,284/M
Quote fraction vs H6 correlationr = −0.13
Invariant violations0

The −0.13 correlation means more verse quotes → slightly less H6 firing, but the effect is weak. H6 fires on the prose context regardless of how much verse is quoted within it. This confirms the pilot finding at scale: H6 is a prose detector, not a verse detector or a "non-scripture" detector.
