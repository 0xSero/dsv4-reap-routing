Experiment 1: Multi-Translation Bible Test

Does H6 depend on which translation is used? 30 passages × 12 translations, all 0-digit controlled. Result: H6 is translation-invariant for verse-formatted translations. MSG (prose paraphrase) at 1,033/M confirms H6 detects format.

1. Design

30 Bible passages spanning Genesis through Romans, each rendered in 12 English translations. All verse numbers stripped. Digit density verified at 0.0000% across all 360 records.

3. The Message (MSG) outlier

MSG fires at 1,033/M — 6–60× higher than any other translation. This is not a failure of translation invariance; it is the strongest confirmation of the format hypothesis. The Message is a conversational paraphrase that abandons verse lineation for flowing prose. From the router's perspective, MSG is prose, not verse. H6 fires on it the same way it fires on commentary prose.

8 of 30 passages show H6=0 across all 12 translations including MSG — the most tightly verse-structured passages where even MSG retains enough verse-like formatting.

5. Key findings

  1. H6 near-zero on all verse-formatted translations. KJV and ESV: perfect zero across 99,765 tokens. Other 10: 17–171/M, negligible vs 163,284/M commentary.
  2. MSG confirms format hypothesis. Fires at 1,033/M — not a bad translation, but prose-format.
  3. Translation invariance holds for verse. KJV (1611) and ESV (2001) both fire at 0.0. Register, era, and translation philosophy are irrelevant.
  4. Digit confound controlled. e164 at 1.6/M. All 360 records 0.0000% digits.

6. Implications

  1. H6 is a format detector. The MSG result is decisive: abandon verse format → H6 fires. Preserve verse lineation → H6 stays near zero.
  2. Combined with Exp 4b and Exp 12: H6 confirmed across 933 records and 6,207,903 tokens — the strongest routing effect in the study.
